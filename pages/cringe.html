Red-winged blackbird

We stopped at a historic Russian outpost for Rest and emptied the trash we had accumulated. The woman who worked the counter knew nothing of the local area, she commuted from the inland for work since none could afford to live where we stood. There was an Eastern Orthodox church, with a small decaying icon of our Lord behind poorly maintained glass. We made our way to the waters cliff and felt the joy and awe of the vast sea. A bench was placed at the cliffs edge, what we called the best bench in the world. Coming back from this wonder, in the tall grass I saw a small bird. Like a raven but too small to be one, and I passively wondered who I was in the company of. As we walked they moved to be away from us and in an instant a flash of vibrant red that vanished. A red wing blackbird.

Hope

Sometimes I have good weeks where I can see clearly the beauty of my life. My wonderful partner who cares and loves for me, the curious joy of encountering others unlike myself, the abundance and wealth I have obtained. At these times the reminder that all things shall be well, all manor of thing shall be well feels like an obvious truism unworthy of speech. These are the times in my life when I learn new skills, find community and learn to love myself better than I had. Every moment feels as though I am on the edge of a cliff ready to jump into a pool of running water, to burst from under the waters surface with only the thought to do it again in my head. A different sort of joyful suicidality comes to the surface and I want to jump of cliffs, skate down big hills, exhilarate and exhaust myself.

It is of course not lost on me in these moments that what I am feeling is temporary, and every grasp towards fulfillment in this state is futile. I have learned in these decades however that if joy is water in my hands, then so is pain. All things I can have are fleeting and cyclical and I can do nothing but ride the waves of tears and excited screams. My true extreme sport is an emotional one whose skill is in surrender, and while I am bad at this skill I have learned much.

Depression

I've been told by my doctors that I suffer from depression. Through counceling and talking with my loves ones I've come to the realization that I am an unreliable narrator of myself. When I am in an episode as I am now, I know that I am a depressive. When I am not, it is something that I feel completely blind to however. It is as if the memory of this moment will be completely wiped from my mind. Though I will remember generally that I was sad, and I will remember the events that occured during that time, the subjective experience of those events will be lost. Even with that I am not sure I even want to believe that I am a depressive.

As I can see it, I can narrate myselff in two ways. In the first I am lazy, lethargic, self centeredly sad, incapable and incompetent. In the second I have a disorder that causes chronic depression and the ailment leaves me sick and often incapable of leading a normal life. I don't know which narrative to adopt. The medical narrative works to defang the moral and value judgements I place upon myself and potentially provide me relief from my ever present omni-critic. That demon that knows all, is allways present, and always wispering my faults. If she may be rendered powerless the I could be free within myself to find joy and love with and in myself.

I feel however that I would lose greatly with her loss. That demon who causes me pain also rightly reminds me to run when my own faults may lead me to a far greater pain. Do I for instance care about my status and economic standing? I do not believe I do, I want nothing and care for no ones praise. However that demon who delights in my suffering also knows that I must not die else she will have no one to pain. She will remind me of all of pain the world has to offer for those who cannot overcome their own defficiencies. Homelessness, disregard, medical decay, poverty and death.

How far would I fall without her selfish love?

Peace

One night I fled. I packed a small amount of clothing and items and moved to a small town about 40 minutes South. I had never owned a car and did not know how to drive, so I found someone to pick me up at a scheduled time when my spouse was away. It has been ten years, some of which was beautiful but most of which had been brutal. I was not blameless, but I was too tired to continue and had deprived myself of joy for so long that my mind was wandering into dangerous thoughts. I moved in with roommates and found a friend who was willing to talk to someone who had nothing but trauma for them. He would not know, but the small act of listening kept me afloat when I had no one.

I was not immediately saved by leaving. The anxiety and fear followed me. Every day was a battle with myself. A battle not to panic in the grocery story, a battle to want good for myself. The pain that I had left was chronic, but the pain of being alone with only my demons and without at least the comfort of nominal love was an acute and intense pain I had not felt. I became desperate and suicidal, alcoholic and promiscuous. While these vices and desires eased my pain, they also put me into contact with new life long wounds.

Eventually I regained employment and started the long work of rebuilding my life. Two years after I had left I found myself in my own apartment. For the time in my life as a 28 year old, I lived alone. My apartment was small and warm. It was winter and snow was on the ground. I had a potpourri of lemons and cranberries on the stove, fogging the windows. A light tune was playing and I was dancing.

Weight

The thought of failure and death is as sweet as the thought of peace and gentleness. Who is more gentle that than which pushes towards nothing? An innate fear and dread of that nothing does not serve me, but serves others through me. I am then only a vessel to be filled and drank out of without myself having anything to drink. At times the wine is sweet and at others bitter. If I may be filled with sweet wine for the pouring out for others, then how lucky am I that I may have nothing to myself. If I am filled with bitter wine then neither myself nor my purpose are fulfilled, and then where is my peace?

Abraham was not thirsty and his wine was bitter. He was fulfilled in traveling up Moriah with only the conviction to murder a child in his heart. Three days he contemplated and resolved himself to murder Isaac. Three days the drained the wells of all the sustained him on this earth, all his hopes all his dreams, all that he had worked towards. Three days he looked at this child, the fruit of his life, and only saw death. Yet he still knew that he would be sustained.

"Jesus answered, 'Everyone who drinks this water will be thirsty again,
but whoever drinks the water I give them will never thirst.
Indeed, the water I give them will become in them
a spring of water welling up to eternal life.'"

Why does it seem that some people are gifted with this ability and others are not. Some may be capable of resigning themselves in faith without a resolution to their suffering, others may be capable of truely believing that they will not suffer through their suffering, and for others suffering is immediate and no resignation can be found. I find no comfort here. I do not know how to resign into discomfort.

Eastern Redbud Tree

There is abundance, hidden in plain sight. Arkansas is the land of forgotten abundance and joy. Every spring the streets blush with pink inviting the town to share a table, but most do not remember how to listen. The flowers of the Eastern redbud are snappy and midly sweet, giving off a bright neon pigment that brings me joy when mixed with a small amount of acid. In early fall the pods form and peas grow like lentils. Each tree provides more than one could eat, and yet no one eats. Here as with the Ancient PawPaw we have forgotten who feeds us and who cares for us. Yet that gracious mother never stops providing and never fails to decorates our home so that we may still see beauty.
